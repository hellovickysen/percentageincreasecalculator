A reverse percentage calculation finds the original value before an increase. The key is to divide by the growth multiplier.

<a class="tool-link" href="/#calculator">Select “Find original” in the calculator</a>

## Reverse percentage formula

```text
Original value = New value ÷ (1 + Percentage increase ÷ 100)
```

If a price is $138 after a 15% increase, the multiplier is 1.15.

```text
$138 ÷ 1.15 = $120
```

The original price was **$120**. You can check it: 15% of $120 is $18, and $120 + $18 is $138.

## Why subtracting 15% is wrong

Subtracting 15% of $138 gives $117.30, not $120. The increase was calculated from the smaller original value. A percentage increase and an equal percentage decrease use different baselines.

This is why a 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease does not return to the starting value. Read the full explanation in [why equal increases and decreases do not cancel](/guides/percentage-increase-decrease-do-not-cancel/).

## Another example with attendance

An event reports 2,640 visitors after attendance increased by 10%.

```text
Original attendance = 2,640 ÷ 1.10 = 2,400
```

The increase was 240 visitors.

## Special cases

A 0% increase means the original and new values are the same. A positive increase always produces a multiplier greater than 1. If the question involves a decrease, the reverse formula uses `1 − decrease rate` instead.

For ordinary forward calculations, see [how to calculate a price increase](/guides/price-increase-percentage/) or [a salary increase percentage](/guides/salary-increase-percentage/).

## Remember the multiplier

Convert the percentage to a multiplier, then divide the final value by that multiplier. Do not subtract the stated percentage from the final value.
