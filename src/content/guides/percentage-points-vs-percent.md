Percentage points and percent increase describe different changes. The distinction matters whenever the values being compared are already percentages.

## Direct difference means percentage points

Suppose a rate rises from 4% to 6%.

```text
6% − 4% = 2 percentage points
```

This is the direct distance between the two rates.

## Relative change means percent increase

To measure the same move relative to the original 4% rate:

```text
((6 − 4) ÷ 4) × 100 = 50%
```

The rate rose by **2 percentage points**, which is a **50% increase** relative to its former level. Both statements are correct, but they answer different questions.

## A survey example

Support for an option moves from 40% to 46%.

- Percentage-point change: 46% − 40% = 6 points
- Percent increase: 6 ÷ 40 × 100 = 15%

Saying “support increased 6%” is ambiguous. “Six percentage points” identifies the direct change. “A 15% increase” identifies the relative change.

## When to use each term

Use **percentage points** when comparing two rates, shares, margins, or percentages directly. Use **percent increase** when describing the size of a change relative to its starting value.

## State both when clarity matters

Reporting both measures prevents a small point change from sounding misleadingly large—or a large relative change from sounding unimportant.
